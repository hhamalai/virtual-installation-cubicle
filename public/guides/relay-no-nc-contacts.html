Relay NO vs NC contacts explained

A relay is an electrically operated switch. A coil creates a magnetic field that moves one or more contacts. The two contact types are normally-open (NO) and normally-closed (NC) — "normal" meaning the resting state with the coil de-energized.

ContactCoil off (rest)Coil on (energized)
Normally open (NO)Open — no currentClosed — conducts
Normally closed (NC)Closed — conductsOpen — no current

The coil: A1 and N

In the simulator each relay has a control coil wired across A1 and N. Energize A1 and connect N back to neutral and the coil pulls in, changing every contact at once. A small indicator lights when the coil is energized.

Two-pole relays: NO-NO, NO-NC and NC-NC

The relays here are two-pole: contact terminals 1–2 form the first pole and 3–4 form the second. The three variants set what each pole does when the coil energizes:

  • NO-NO — both poles close when energized.
  • NO-NC — pole 1 closes, pole 2 opens (useful for switching one load on while switching another off).
  • NC-NC — both poles open when energized.

Try it: switch a lamp with a relay

  1. Wire line L to relay terminal 1, and terminal 2 to the lamp's L. Lamp N goes back to the source.
  2. Wire the coil: L to A1, and the relay's N to the source neutral.
  3. On an NO relay the lamp is off until the coil energizes, then pole 1 closes and the lamp lights. On an NC relay it is the opposite.
