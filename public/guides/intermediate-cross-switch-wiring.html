Intermediate (cross) switch wiring: control a light from three or more places

To control one lamp from three or more locations you keep a two-way switch at each end and add an intermediate switch — also called a cross or four-way switch — in the middle of the two travellers. Each intermediate switch either passes the travellers straight through or swaps them over, which flips the circuit. This guide shows the connections and lets you build it in a free browser simulator.

Open the simulator →

What you need

  • Two changeover switches (the Type 6 switch in the simulator)
  • One intermediate/cross switch per extra location (the Type 7 switch)
  • One lamp and a line/neutral power source

The terminals

The intermediate Type 7 switch has IN1, IN2, OUT1, OUT2. In one position IN1→OUT1 and IN2→OUT2 (straight through); in the other IN1→OUT2 and IN2→OUT1 (crossed). It always sits between the travellers of the two two-way switches.

Step by step

  1. Connect line L to COM of the first Type 6 switch.
  2. Connect that switch's L1 and L2 to the intermediate switch IN1 and IN2.
  3. Connect the intermediate switch OUT1 and OUT2 to L1 and L2 of the second Type 6 switch.
  4. Connect COM of the second switch to the lamp's L, and the lamp's N back to the source N.

Add more intermediate switches in series along the travellers for four, five or more switch positions. Any switch you flip toggles the lamp.
