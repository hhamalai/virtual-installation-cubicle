How to wire a two-way switch (control a light from two places)

A two-way switch — also called a changeover switch — lets you turn one lamp on or off from two separate locations, such as the top and bottom of a staircase. It has three terminals: a common (COM) and two travellers (L1 and L2). This guide walks through the wiring and lets you build it in a free browser simulator.

Open the simulator →

What you need

  • Two changeover switches (the Type 6 switch in the simulator)
  • One lamp
  • A line/neutral power source

The terminals

Each Type 6 switch has COM, L1 and L2. In one position COM connects to L1; in the other, COM connects to L2. The two L1/L2 wires running between the switches are the travellers.

Step by step

  1. Connect the line L from the power source to COM on switch A.
  2. Connect switch A L1 to switch B L1, and switch A L2 to switch B L2 — these are the two travellers.
  3. Connect COM on switch B to the lamp's L terminal.
  4. Connect the lamp's N back to the power source N.

Toggle either switch: whenever COM on switch A and COM on switch B are linked through the same traveller, the circuit is complete and the lamp lights. Flip either switch and the path breaks. That is exactly how stairway lighting works.
